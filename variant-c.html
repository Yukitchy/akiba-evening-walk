Three Ways to Walk

One evening in Akihabara, three possible stories — the deep dive, the market road, or the road to the book town. Choose the one that sounds like you.

Walks by Yuuki, local guide16:00–21:00 · About 5 hours

Akihabara after dark. Every route starts here at 16:00 — they simply end in different Tokyos.

None of these is the shopping tour. Each route walks the history and culture of the town at an unhurried pace, from the last of the daylight into the night streets. They share a start, a guide and a philosophy; they differ in where the story goes. Read all three, and pick the evening you would rather have.

Route A

Akihabara in Depth

One district, read deeply

How did a black market become Electric Town, and Electric Town the otaku capital? This route stays inside Akihabara and answers that in one place, layer by layer — ending at the main street just as the neon reaches its peak.

KANDA MYOJIN AT DUSK → BACK STREETS → UNDER THE TRACKS → PARTS ALLEYS → CHUO-DORI NEON → RADIO KAIKAN → DINNER

  • The shrine on the hill. Kanda Myojin, founded in 730 and moved to this hill in 1616 to guard Edo Castle, still watches over the neighbourhood — and now blesses laptops as happily as festivals.
  • The birth of Electric Town. Postwar stalls sold radio parts around the station; in 1949 the sellers were moved under the railway arches, and their maze still trades today.
  • Neon at its peak. Appliances, then computers, then anime and games — one street, three eras, read as the lights come on.

About 3 km, finishing in Akihabara. Best for a first evening in Akihabara, and for anime or game fans who want the story behind the shops.

Route B

Akiba → Okachimachi → Ameyoko

Electric Town to market town

This route walks north along the railway, from the electronics quarter into the Tokyo of markets, makers and drinking lanes — the everyday city that most tours never reach.

AKIHABARA → SUEHIROCHO → 2K540 ARTISAN ARCHES → OKACHIMACHI → AMEYOKO MARKET → IZAKAYA LANES

  • Ameyoko, while it is awake. The market began as a postwar black market by Ueno Station; candy stalls made it “Candy Store Alley”, and American-goods shops near Okachimachi added “America Alley” before the two merged. Its shops start closing around 19:00–20:00, so the market honestly comes first.
  • 2k540. Artisan studios inside the arches between Akihabara and Okachimachi — named for a railway distance marker, 2 km 540 m from Tokyo Station.
  • The lanes light up. As the market shutters fall, the izakaya under the tracks switch on. The second half of the evening belongs to them.

About 4 km, finishing on the Okachimachi–Ueno side. Best for food-and-market people, repeat visitors, and anyone who prefers everyday local Tokyo to otaku culture.

Route C

Akiba → Kanda → Jimbocho

Old Tokyo, page by page

The quietest of the three: west into old Kanda, through a vanished station, and on to the book town — including the face Jimbocho only shows after its shops have closed.

KANDA MYOJIN → MANSEIBASHI & mAAch → KANDA STATION QUARTER → UNDER-TRACK LANES → JIMBOCHO BOOK STREETS → THE QUIET BOOK TOWN

  • The station that disappeared. Manseibashi Station, opened in 1912 to Tatsuno Kingo’s design, closed in 1943; its red-brick viaduct survives as mAAch ecute. Coffee inside the arches.
  • Old Kanda. One of Edo’s merchant and craftsman quarters; around Kanda Station, the old eateries and under-track lanes still carry that feeling.
  • Jimbocho, open and closed. Around 130 secondhand bookshops gathered as the Meiji-era universities arrived. We browse while they are open — then walk the shuttered, lamplit street most visitors never see.

About 4 km, finishing in Jimbocho. Best for book lovers and history lovers, and anyone who prefers the calm of old Tokyo to the neon.

Good to know

  • January evenings are cold — bring a warm layer; we are outside for much of the walk.
  • Closing times shape each route: small parts shops and market stalls wind down around 19:00–20:00, while big stores, arcades and restaurants stay open late — each route is sequenced around that.
  • Bring some cash — small stalls, market shops and shrine charms are often cash only.
  • Shrine steps: routes A and C include one flight of steps at Kanda Myojin.
  • Photos: your guide takes photos along the way and shares an album afterwards.
